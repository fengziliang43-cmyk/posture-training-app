Can you run with scoliosis?

Yes, most people with scoliosis can run. The NHS says most people with scoliosis can do most activities, including exercise and sport, and SOSORT recommends general sport for its physical and psychological benefits. Start gradually, let your back's response guide you, and check with your doctor first if you've had spinal surgery or your curve has been changing.

How to start

  • Begin with walk-run intervals: for example, 1 minute of easy running and 2 minutes of walking, repeated 6 to 8 times.
  • Run on even ground at first, in shoes that feel comfortable.
  • Do two or three sessions a week, with a rest day in between.
  • Add a little each week, such as one more interval or one more minute of running, rather than doubling your distance.

Check how your back responds

Rate your back discomfort from 0 to 10 before and after each run. Mild stiffness that settles within a day is common. If discomfort is 2 or more points higher afterwards, or still raised the next day, go back to the previous week's amount.

Strength helps your running

Two short strength sessions a week support your trunk and hips. The NHS recommends strengthening activities for all the major muscle groups on at least 2 days a week. Our home routine for adults with scoliosis is a good place to start.

Check with a doctor first if

  • You've had spinal fusion surgery: your surgeon will tell you when and how to start.
  • Your curve has been getting bigger on recent X-rays.
  • Running brings on numbness, tingling or pain that shoots down a leg. Stop, and read the red flags guide.
